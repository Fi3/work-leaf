## What qualifies as a later new issue or discovery

An unexpected issue is a concrete, previously unknown failure or external-state
change, with dated evidence and its affected existing task. A promising new cause
requires a previously unknown fact, an upstream decision/work pathway plausibly
large enough to matter, and a cheap discriminating check. Name the exact new fact,
why it was not known when this list was frozen, related prior hypotheses/results,
potential explanatory reach, and the proposed check's factor set and stop gate.
A favorable total, unexplained residual or mere possibility is not that evidence.

Known unfinished setup, known weak/parked candidates, already-described joint
hypotheses, routine failure handling and omitted known follow-up work are not new
discoveries. A newly established fact may change a parked candidate's priority,
but must be identified explicitly; simply renaming its old hypothesis does not.
A missed known requirement is an initial-checklist omission, openly reported as
case (2), not relabeled as an unexpected discovery.

New findings are recorded immediately and may re-rank work already inside the
approved scope. This exception is not blanket permission to increment TODO,
change a frozen contrast/budget, retry a provider observation or execute extra
work. A necessary extension still names its exact tasks/count and requests user
approval before the counter or execution scope grows. Preserve the user's zero
