## Stage A: comprehensive candidate map

Inspect actual WL source, the saved Direct driver and relevant historical evidence. Cover every
boundary that can change model-visible inputs, generated outputs, response count or retained
conversation state. Audit these families in parallel without provider generation:

- Source/context delivery: initial reads, large-read bundles, repeated-read reminders and diffs,
  request merging, snapshots, invalidation, refreshes, command output and reconstruction work.
- Instructions and work selection: validation scope, stop/retry behavior, completion guidance,
  patch granularity, edit representation, automatic apply/commit work, review and fix routing.
- Workflow and provider lifecycle: launch/resume policy, session reuse, tool availability,
  interruption/continuation, hidden calls, review/linearization, scheduling, locks and conflicts.
- Accounting and shared factors: caching, compaction, cumulative versus per-response usage,
  failed/retried work and capture coverage. Separate measurement artifacts from genuine savings.

The deliverable is [CANDIDATE-MAP.md](CANDIDATE-MAP.md), with a source-coverage matrix and named
independently meaningful factors. Related factors may share a family entry, provided each distinct
subfactor and its isolated test boundary remain explicit. The original [candidate list](CANDIDATES.md) and
[priority notes](DESIGN-MECHANISM-PRIORITIES.md) are inputs, not an exhaustive inventory or an active
ranking. Add overlooked factors, merge duplicates and record interactions explicitly.

Each candidate needs:

1. Exact paths, symbols and the call/configuration chain; identify the source version.
2. The actual WL-versus-Direct difference, or precise evidence still needed to establish it.
3. The action/context → response/input/output → raw-token causal chain, including possible
   offsetting work. A shorter prompt alone is not proof of lower whole-workflow usage.
4. Existing exposure and test evidence, including null results, failures and parked runs.
5. Separate labels for hypothesis, observed exposure, demonstrated local mechanism, demonstrated
   net saving, not exposed in a named cohort, shared behavior, and tested-but-not-demonstrated.
6. The smallest safe single-factor intervention and non-target behavior that must stay fixed.
7. Overlaps with other factors and the next evidence needed, without contribution percentages.

Coverage gate: each family and relevant model-delivery/lifecycle boundary has an inspected source
entry, a candidate or an explicit exclusion/shared-factor rationale. No pending audit section may
be silently called complete. Completeness refers to the inspected WL/Direct implementation and
workflow, not arbitrary hidden provider behavior. Rank evidence needs only after this broad pass;
do not pick one attractive example and abandon the rest.

## Stage B: identify mechanisms that actually save tokens

Use existing traces and comparable saved WL baselines first. For unresolved candidates, specify
isolated benchmark-only interventions and verify actual exposure, non-target identity and the
downstream response/context chain. Establish whether removing a mechanism increases raw usage;
directional token evidence is necessary here, but contribution percentages are not the objective.

Keep theoretical possibilities distinct from exercised mechanisms and measured net savings.
A narrow cue's null result does not exclude a whole policy family. An inactive compactor cannot
explain that cohort's saving, even if it might save tokens on another workload. Repeated retained
input is a cost channel shared by several causes, not another independent cause to count twice.

Repeat promising modified variants when evidence warrants confirmation. Before any generation,
record the exact factor, fixed run count, retained baseline provenance, sources and stop condition.
Capacity does not authorize extra runs, automatic replacements or a new control arm. Reuse saved
read treatments at the appropriate point in the broad investigation; do not regenerate them merely
because their analysis was parked.

Exit evidence is a disposition for every mapped candidate: supported saving mechanism, tested but
not demonstrated, not exposed in the relevant cohort, shared/non-causal factor, or an explicit
unresolved item with a concrete next check. Unresolved plausible causes remain work, not zeroes.

## Stage C: quantify contributions after mechanism identification

For supported mechanisms, estimate magnitude using repeated modified runs and the existing baseline
with a common accounting scope. Distinguish a factor's marginal WL ablation effect from its share of
the historical Direct–WL gap; these are not interchangeable denominators.

Measure relevant interactions or state an explicit attribution convention where shares overlap.
Smaller input and fewer continuations affect the same repeated-context cost. Do not sum independent
one-factor differences into a fabricated 100%, conceal negative offsets or assign an unresolved
remainder to a favorite mechanism. Preserve uncertainty without midpoint or zero-fill.

The final deliverable connects source behavior, actual exposure, downstream actions, exact
response/item usage and supported contribution estimates. A positive single candidate does not
finish the investigation when other substantial causes remain unresolved.
